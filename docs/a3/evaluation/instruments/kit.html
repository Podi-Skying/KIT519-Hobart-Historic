Before each session

  1. Open the prototype in a new private/incognito tab. This clears saved language, text size, stops and likes.
  2. Set the phone to portrait, sound on, brightness high. Decide on location permission (allow or deny) and note it on §5.
  3. Write the participant ID (P1…Pn), role (P1 Minzi, P2 Margaret, P3 Sam) and build commit on §5.
  4. Moderator reads the script (§4) word for word and never demonstrates. Any hint counts as an assist.

Signed consent forms, raw notes and any recordings go in docs/a3/evaluation/private/ (git-ignored) or stay on paper. Only anonymised data is entered into data/*.csv.

§1

Information sheet & consent

What is this?

Group 13 (KIT519, University of Tasmania) has designed a prototype app that helps people explore Hobart's heritage sites on foot. We want to find out where the design is easy or hard to use. We are testing the app, not you. There are no right or wrong answers.

What will you do?

For about 35 minutes you will imagine you are a particular person (we'll give you a card), try a few tasks on a phone while saying what you think out loud, and answer short questionnaires.

Your data

  • Participation is voluntary. You can stop at any time without giving a reason, and ask for your data to be removed.
  • We record what happens on screen, the time taken and what you say. We do not record your name with your answers; you will be "P" plus a number.
  • We will only record the screen or audio if you agree below. Recordings are kept privately and deleted after the unit is graded.
  • Anonymised results (for example times, scores and short quotes) may appear in our assignment report and presentation.

Persona role cards (cut out; hand one to the participant)

You are Minzi, 22: an exchange student from Seoul

You're in Hobart for a semester and love old buildings (you study design). You hurt your leg, so hills and long walks are painful. You read English slowly and prefer Korean. Today is Saturday and you want to visit one heritage place.

You are Margaret, 71: a Sandy Bay resident

Your eyesight isn't what it was: small or pale text is hard to read. You walk with a stick and avoid steep streets. You like to know where the toilets and cafés are, and you prefer something on paper to carry. You've never used AR.

You are Sam, 38: a Year 9 history teacher

You're planning a two-hour convict-history excursion this week for 24 students. One student uses a wheelchair. Students can't rely on phones, so you want a printed hand-out with the route and facts about each stop.

Role-play note: participants are asked to answer "as the persona would", but to report their own difficulties honestly. Record in §5 whether the participant genuinely shares any persona trait (e.g. is a Korean speaker, uses larger text).

§4

Moderator script

Thanks for helping. We're testing a prototype of a Hobart heritage walking app. Remember, we're testing the app, not you. If something is confusing, that's exactly what we need to know.

I'll give you a card describing who you are. Please try each task as that person would. As you go, think aloud: tell me what you're looking at, what you expect, and what you're trying to do.

Let's practise: tell me what you think this screen is for. (Show Home for 30 s, then continue.)

I can't help during the tasks, because I want to see how the app works on its own. If you get stuck, try what you would normally do. You can say "I'd give up" at any time.

For each task: hand over the task card and read it aloud. Start the timer at the first touch. Neutral prompts only: "What are you looking for?", "What do you expect to happen?". Stop at success, at the time limit, or when the participant gives up. Then ask the SEQ (§5).

(If stuck for 60 s:) What would you try next? If still stuck after the time limit, show the path, and log it as failed.

(After the last task:) Please fill in this short questionnaire about the whole app (§6). Then I have a few questions (§7).

Heuristic evaluation form (Nielsen, 1994; complete independently before merging)

Evaluator: E__ · Date: ______ · Walk through every screen in the site map at least twice: once freely, once following each persona's workflows.

HeuristicPrompts for this app
H1 Visibility of system statusRoute loading and "straight-line estimate"; location state; which route type, stops and language are active; arrival.
H2 Match with the real worldWalking times and distances people can trust; street and place names; "Accessible" meaning; nipaluna / kunanyi naming.
H3 User control and freedomUndo a stop, change route type or mode mid-walk, close sheets, get back from AR, collapse and restore the map panel.
H4 Consistency and standardsSame minutes for the same trip on every screen; one action colour; same arrival behaviour in map and AR.
H5 Error preventionStop limit (4); unknown site links; routes that include steps; printing an empty route.
H6 Recognition rather than recallIcon-only buttons (voice, offline, locate); where the text-size setting lives; stop icons.
H7 Flexibility and efficiencyShortcuts (nearest site, weather → plan); expert paths; search vs filter.
H8 Aesthetic and minimalist designDuplicate content (Top 5 vs all 5 sites); panel density at larger text; one primary button per screen.
H9 Recognise, diagnose, recover from errorsNo route found; speech voice missing; location denied or outside Hobart.
H10 Help and documentationAR help overlay; route-type explanations; first-use guidance.
